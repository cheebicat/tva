My name is Luana Cerna, and I'm a MtF trans musician. My primary instruments are piano and alto saxophone. While I've always loved music, I've always been uncomfortable with my voice. <!--more--> I've gotten my speaking voice to a point I'm mostly happy with, but I honestly don't know how far I've gotten with my singing voice. I feel like I *have* to pass, even though I wish I could feel otherwise. Speaking in public is unavoidable, so I focused very hard on my speaking voice. On the other hand, I was able to just avoid singing in public, but that also means I've never really gotten validation for it. Despite my mixed emotions with my voice, I still love expressing myself by singing. I feel I'm able to express my raw emotions better than I can on piano or sax, even if I'm only expressing them to myself. I feel like I only able to like my singing voice in spite of my trans identity, and I admire those who can proudly embrace it and show it off.

In general, I try to push myself to be as transparently feminine as possible. This can be seen in the way I treat my voice, and it also carries over to the way I treat myself as a musician. I know there are better ways to embrace being trans, but I am not a person who can confidently and unapologetically present my true self. Instead, I found quiet ways to validate my femininity through music. Back in 3rd grade, I actually wanted to learn flute at first, but my parents told me that it was too girly. I ended up playing alto sax instead, which I was afraid was too boyish, but one of my saxophone seniors was a girl, and I was so relieved thinking "oh, it's okay for girls to play sax too". I also worked on playing piano more expressively since I associated it with femininity. I gravitated toward pieces with more rubato and didn't like it when pieces were really strict with tempo. Back when I didn't know I was trans, I was always subconsciously trying to find small victories like these.

Nowadays, I don't really chase femininity in my musical performance. I'm still not entirely confident in myself, but I do my best to view being trans as just another part of me. It undeniably influences the way I think about and experience music, but I don't want to focus on it so much that it becomes all that identifies me. I'm quite happy with how I am as a trans musician because it feels like a natural part of me instead of something I'm forcing.
